What Prognos Labs is about

Prognos Labs is a prediction market with AI models built in. Every market asks one question about what happens next, with two outcomes. You follow the outcome you think will happen, and the price shows the chance traders give it. A price of 62% means the market, as a whole, puts the chance at about 62 in 100. The price is never set by us. It moves with every trade, as traders buy and sell and the balance of opinion shifts.

Most prediction markets show you a price and leave you to guess what it means. Prognos Labs does more. Every market carries a forecast from its AI models, with a plain-language reason for it. The models read the live price move, the headlines, the mood, the order book and the time left, and they tell you, in words, what the market looks like and what comes next. They never tell you which side to pick. And they keep learning. Every time a market settles, the forecast it gave is scored against the real outcome, so the next forecast is sharper than the last. That is the whole point of Prognos Labs: a market that does not just show you the odds, it helps you read them.

Right now the markets are short crypto moves: up or down over the next few minutes. New questions arrive through the day, and the forecast is live on every one of them.

How a market works

Each market asks one question and offers two outcomes. For a crypto market the question is whether the price closes up or down over a set window, five or fifteen minutes. Traders place bids and asks on each outcome, and those orders form the order book. The best bid is the highest price anyone will pay for an outcome right now. The best ask is the lowest price anyone will sell it for. The gap between them is the spread, and the last trade price is the price of the most recent trade.

The order book

The order book shows every open bid and ask for each outcome, best first. A deep book, with large sizes near the top, means many traders are willing to trade close to the current price. A thin book means one trade can move the price further. The market page shows the full book for both outcomes, so the depth behind the price is always visible. Nothing about the price is hidden.

What you get on every market

  • A forecast on every market, with a plain-language reason, so you never have to guess what the price means.
  • The full order book on both sides, so you can see the depth behind the price before you trust it.
  • Price alerts that flag a market when the price jumps, the spread widens or the quote goes stale.
  • A watchlist that keeps the markets you care about in one place, with their price, time left and forecast.
  • Market news, tagged by asset, so you can see which headlines touch the markets listed here.
  • Short windows, five or fifteen minutes, so a market you follow is decided quickly and you are never left waiting days for an answer.

How a market settles

When the event ends, the market settles on the real outcome, from the source stated on the market page. A person confirms the outcome, based on the real event the question describes. The side that called it right is the winning side. The forecast never settles a market.

How the forecast is worked out

Every market carries a forecast. It is a short note on what the market looks like right now, written from the market itself, in four plain steps. Nothing is guessed, and nothing is hidden: you can check each step against the market yourself.

The price move

The forecast reads how the asset has moved, from the live price feed. A sharp move in a day, or over the last hour, is the first thing it notes. When the asset has barely moved, it says that too.

The headlines and the mood

It reads the latest headlines about the asset and the mood across the news in the last hour. When the headlines are heavy, it says so. When there is no news about the asset, it says nothing about news, rather than inventing a story.

The order book

It looks at the money sitting in the book, but it only names a level when it is big enough to matter, at least $1,000 at one price. A thin book is skipped, never dressed up as a wall, because a few hundred dollars cannot push a prediction market.

The time left

On a market with only a few minutes left, the next move decides it. With hours or days left, there is room for the picture to change. The forecast weighs the time left on every market it reads, and ends on what comes next for the asset.

The forecast is a read of the market, not a promise about the outcome. It never tells you which side to pick. It never creates a market and it never decides one.

How the forecast learns

The forecast does not stay fixed. Every time the page loads, it writes down the forecast it showed for each market: the chance it gave, the price, the spread, the time left and the volume. When a market ends, it checks the real outcome and scores that forecast against it. A forecast that called it right scores well. One that missed does not.

From those scored forecasts it builds a second version of the forecast, called the challenger. The challenger is only a small adjustment to the starting formula, worked out from past results. It is tested on the newest markets first, before it is allowed near the forecast you see. It only replaces the forecast you see when it beats the current one on those newer markets by a clear margin. If it does not, the forecast stays as it is, and the challenger goes back to learning.

Your watchlist, price alerts and market news

Your watchlist

Star any market to keep it on your watchlist. It is saved on your device, so it stays with you when you come back. The watchlist shows the price, the time left and the forecast for each market you watch, so the ones you care about are always one tap away.

Price alerts

The site flags a market when the price moved 5 points or more, when the gap between the best bid and ask widened sharply, or when the quote went stale. When nothing is flagged, nothing shows. Nothing is flagged by hand.

Market news

The news digest, under News in the menu, lists the latest crypto headlines, each with its source, its time and a link to read it. Headlines are tagged by the asset they mention, so you can see at a glance which ones touch the markets listed here. The headlines come from public news feeds and are never written here.
